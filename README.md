# spacetime

A custom atlas of history, built with React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, pnpm and Vite. MapLibre GL JS draws bundled Natural Earth geography. No API key or external tile service is used at runtime.

## Run

Node 20.19+ or Node 22 is recommended. Run `pnpm install`, then `pnpm dev`. `pnpm build` checks types and creates `dist/`; `pnpm preview` serves it. `pnpm test` checks historical selection and timeline bounds.

## Current experience

Playable stories are a classroom entry, not just another timeline bar: search Macedonian Empire or World War II in the top box (or pick one under Key events), land at a starting year with a calibrated zoom, see event dots on the map, and press play. Macedonian Empire runs from Philip II through Alexander’s campaigns to the successor wars, with a 100-year window so Alexander’s lifetime stays a long bar; World War II opens in 1939 on a world map with a 20-year window. Other spans stay on the timeline as chapters. A top bar to travel anywhere: search for a story, person, empire or country, or type a year (BCE/CE) and go. A time scrubber sits directly under the map, joined to it, so you can move through time without scrolling to the timeline: slide the thin overview line in the bar above the map to cross all of history, or drag (or flick, or swipe with two fingers) the ruler under the orange needle for fine steps. It shows the current year (the timeline itself only marks it on its centre line) and shares the timeline’s window, so both always show the same year and zoom; arrow keys, Page Up/Down, Home and End work when it has focus. Its ruler is also the timeline's year axis: it spans exactly the same width as the timeline bars, so the timeline has no separate year header, and the orange needle continues as the timeline's orange line. The − and + at the end of the overview line zoom both. The ruler and the timeline bars run the full width, so that line is the exact horizontal centre of the screen (the timeline's group names are headings above their lanes). Small play controls sit in that bar above the map, next to the overview line: one year back or forward, play the selected story, and jump to its previous or next border change. The Filter button in the year box opens a panel to show everything, only stories or only people, to hide turning points, and to choose which kinds of people appear. Stories/People filters, event markers, border-change playback, independent map/time zoom, year selection across 5000 BCE to today, and date-valid map layers. See [coverage and sources](docs/COVERAGE.md) and [data contract](docs/DATA_MODEL.md).

## Data and historical limitations

`public/data/land.geojson` is the Natural Earth 1:50m land dataset, downloaded from https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/blob/master/geojson/ne_50m_land.geojson on 2026-10-05. It is public domain: https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/. This is modern physical geography, not ancient coastlines. No modern political borders are shown. Coarse detail makes this suitable for world/regional views, not street-scale research.

Persian, Macedonian and Roman territory comes from [Cliopatria](https://github.com/Seshat-Global-History-Databank/cliopatria) v0.2.1 (Seshat Global History Databank, [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/); method paper: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-04516-9). Polygons are kept as published, apart from coordinate rounding to 0.0001° and clipping each row's validity to the span that shows it. Polity names and year ranges are kept too, except for the rows listed in `scripts/cliopatria-corrections.ts`. Each of those corrections renames, re-dates, regroups or splits a row that contradicts the historical record, cites its evidence and never touches geometry. Every import (world tiles, polity catalogue, story polygons) applies them first, so all three agree. Rows of one name that overlap after a correction are merged per year range. Corrected rows carry their note into provenance and the map popup. Each story names the Cliopatria polities (or groupings) it is about, and `src/data/territories.ts` draws their polygons while the story is active; a grouping member is drawn only in the years Cliopatria lists it. For example: Persia → Achaemenid Empire, the Macedonian story → (Macedonian Empire) plus the successor kingdoms, Republic → Roman Republic and Roman Empire (from 31 BCE in Cliopatria), Empire → Roman Empire. Cliopatria samples borders at intervals, so a shape holds until its next row. To refresh, download and unzip `cliopatria.geojson.zip` from that repository into `data-raw/`, then run `pnpm data:world` (world tiles and polity catalogue) and `pnpm data:cliopatria`. The latter extracts into `src/data/cliopatria.json` the rows every curated story span in `src/data/curated.ts` needs. A correction that no longer matches a row stops the import.

Timeline people are lifetimes with dates from [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/) (CC0), best-rank birth/death statements only; decade precision and “circa” become approximate ranges, conflicting values become disputed ranges. Roman emperors are everyone Wikidata lists as holding Roman emperor (Q842606) or Western Roman emperor (Q111841409) starting by 476 CE; those without a known birth year are skipped. Presidents of Brazil are everyone Wikidata lists as holding President of Brazil (Q5176750), from Deodoro da Fonseca to Lula and Jair Bolsonaro, ranked by Pantheon's index like everyone else, with their terms of office in the subtitle. From 5000 BCE, people are the most notable per birth century by [Pantheon](https://pantheon.world/)'s Historical Popularity Index (more per century for recent centuries), so Julius Caesar, Cleopatra, Hammurabi and the early pharaohs appear alongside the emperors. Pantheon's earliest people are from about 4000 BCE; many early rulers have legendary or estimated dates, and those with no usable date in Wikidata or Pantheon are skipped. Everyone Pantheon records as a mathematician with an index of at least 70 is included, from any century, so the line runs from Euclid and Archimedes through Fermat and Pascal into the twentieth century. Thales, Pythagoras, Hipparchus and Eratosthenes are included too; Pantheon classifies those four as a philosopher, a philosopher, an astronomer and a geographer. Each person keeps every facet that matches Pantheon's occupation or a Wikidata occupation (mathematician, philosopher, writer, and the rest), so someone who was both stays both. The timeline starts with every facet on. Turning a facet off hides people who have none of the facets still on. All and None turn every facet on or off at once. Pantheon is CC BY-SA 4.0, so the generated tiles in `public/data/timeline/` are shared under CC BY-SA 4.0. Where Wikidata has no year-precision date, Pantheon's year is used and marked approximate. Living people's bars run to the import year and fade out. `pnpm data:people` regenerates `public/data/timeline/`: an `index.json` plus one validated tile per century. The app fetches only tiles near the visible time window (`src/useTimelineTiles.ts`). The import also writes `people-search.json`, a small index of everyone with their lifetime and place of birth (Wikidata P19, best rank, with the place's coordinates P625); about 97% have a mapped birthplace. The search box loads it on first use and also searches every Cliopatria polity and grouping, matching names ignoring case and accents. Picking someone moves the timeline to their birth year, selects their lifetime and flies the map to their birthplace with a pin. Picking a polity moves the timeline to its largest recorded extent (for a grouping, that of its largest member), outlines it and fits the map to it. Lanes are packed by notability across everyone, so bars stay put while panning; zooming out keeps the top lanes (“+N more” expands them). A filtered set is packed again from the people already loaded. Two-finger horizontal swipe pans the timeline; pinch zooms around the pointer.

The world political map shows every Cliopatria polity in force in the selected year (about 1,500 polities, 3400 BCE–2024 CE; Cliopatria has nothing earlier, so 5000–3400 BCE shows land only, and coverage before 1000 BCE is sparse), with Cliopatria's names and years. Parenthesised Cliopatria groupings are skipped so components are not drawn twice. `pnpm data:world` builds `public/data/world/`: geometry simplified for world-scale display (Douglas–Peucker at 0.03°, rounded to 0.01°, about 1 km), a stable colour per polity name, and a label point inside the largest part (`polylabel`, ISC). Tiles are 100 years before 1500, 50 years to 1750 and 10 years after, because borders change faster; the app loads the tile containing the selected year and prefetches its neighbours (`src/useWorldTiles.ts`). Total is about 48 MB on disk; the largest tile is 2.2 MB (0.6 MB gzipped). Coverage follows Cliopatria: dense for Eurasia, thinner for pre-colonial North America, sub-Saharan Africa and Oceania. This layer is a reference basemap like the land data and does not pass through the dataset schema; story spans keep their own validated geometry, drawn on top with a bold outline. The same import writes `src/data/polities.json` (`pnpm data:polities` rebuilds only that): one entity per map polity, with an ID from its Wikidata item where that item names only this polity and from its name otherwise, plus Cliopatria's parenthesised groupings such as (British Empire), (Warring States China) or (Carolingian Empire). A grouping lists its member polities with the years Cliopatria records them as members; nested groupings are flattened. Alliance and allegiance groupings are left out.

Stories are the only timeline concept for history above a single life: chapters, the broad arcs of history. There is no separate empire layer; Cliopatria has no such category, and a single empire is simply a story about one polity. The seven antiquity chapters keep their events and territory. `src/data/chapters.ts` adds 31 more, from Warring States China to the globalised world, so every year from 550 BCE to today falls inside at least one chapter. Chapter dates are conventional and marked approximate. A story's subjects are polity entities, either single polities or groupings, and stories about the same polity share its entity. While the selected year is inside a story, the world map outlines its polities in that year; a grouping outlines only the members Cliopatria lists for that year. Large polities without a story yet (Xiongnu, the Tsardom of Russia, the Safavids, the steppe khaganates) can still be found by search. All stories share packed lanes in one Stories group. On the timeline, stories are solid bars and people are rounded pills.

History marks (`src/data/marks.ts`) are a short curated list of wars, pandemics and turning points, from the Battle of Marathon to COVID-19, with years from each Wikipedia article's lead. A single-year mark is a dashed vertical line through the whole timeline; a period is a faint band. Their labels sit in a row under the year axis, coloured by kind (war, pandemic, event), packed into up to four lanes; labels that do not fit at the current zoom are hidden until you zoom in, while their lines and bands stay. Clicking a label moves the timeline to the mark's year. They are shown to readers as “Turning points”; the “Turning points” checkbox hides them.

Athens and Sparta are not in Cliopatria as separate polities. Their polygons in `src/data/territories.ts` remain original illustrative sketches, not sourced boundaries.

`src/data/alexander-draft.ts` contains the authored curation input; `src/data/alexander.ts` normalizes it and validates the resulting dataset. Event coordinates are approximate. Text is original paraphrase based on:

- The Metropolitan Museum of Art: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-rise-of-macedonia-and-the-conquests-of-alexander-the-great
- World History Encyclopedia chronology: https://www.worldhistory.org/timeline/Alexander_the_Great/
- UCL, Alexander in Egypt: https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt/chronology/alexandergreat.html

The next historical-data step is sourced geometry for Athens and Sparta, and recording confidence and competing interpretations per feature. Keep temporal selectors separate from the renderer. For multiple stories, add chapter records, event/source IDs and timeline ranges; do not infer unknown territory from a campaign point.

## Mapping recommendation

**MapLibre GL JS + bundled Natural Earth** is the recommended zero-account initial setup. MapLibre is a BSD-3-Clause open-source renderer, not a tile hosting service: https://github.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/blob/main/LICENSE.txt and https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/. Keep its packaged license notices when distributing. Local development and the bundled map require no paid service; website hosting and bandwidth are separate and are not guaranteed free.

Natural Earth is public domain and can be redistributed with the app. Rendering the local land dataset avoids third-party tile quotas, API keys, style/font services, and the standard Google Maps appearance. The tradeoff is a ~2 MB geography asset, limited detail, and WebGL support requirements.

Alternatives: Leaflet is BSD-2-Clause (https://github.com/Leaflet/Leaflet/blob/main/LICENSE), a good lightweight choice for raster maps and GeoJSON, but hosted tiles still need a provider. OpenLayers is BSD-2-Clause (https://github.com/openlayers/openlayers/blob/main/LICENSE.md), strong for GIS formats/projections but more than this foundation needs. OpenStreetMap data is ODbL (https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright); its public tile service is capacity-limited and policy-governed, not an unlimited free CDN. See https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/ before choosing that service. This project does not call it.

For higher-resolution future maps, evaluate self-hosted vector tiles/PMTiles and their underlying data licenses, storage and bandwidth costs separately. Do not use demonstration tile endpoints as a production entitlement.

## Structure

- `src/MapScrubber.tsx`: the time scrubber under the map.
- `src/FilterMenu.tsx`: the timeline Filter panel.
- `src/Atlas.tsx`: map lifecycle, local basemap, layers, markers and camera controls.
- `src/domain/schema.ts`: Zod domain schema, runtime validation and era conversion.
- `src/domain/selectors.ts`: dataset-independent time selection and GeoJSON projection.
- `src/data/`: Alexander curation input and normalization adapter.
- `src/main.tsx`: app, timeline, playback and source notes.
- `src/style.css`: visual system with Tailwind import and responsive component styles. The look is aimed at schools: plain sentence-case labels, nothing smaller than 11px so it projects well, and [Lexend](https://www.lexend.com/) (SIL Open Font License), a typeface designed for reading fluency, bundled from `@fontsource-variable/lexend`.

All runtime map requests stay on the app origin. External references open only when clicked. There is no analytics, remote font dependency or persisted user data.
